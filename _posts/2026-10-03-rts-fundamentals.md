## The Nature of Fundamentals
In [Adjust your expectations]({% post_url 2026-10-03-before-you-start-the-game %}#adjust-your-expectations), we learned that we must learn to walk before we can run. Fundamentals are the "simple" building blocks that more "complex" skills are made up of. The quotation marks around "simple" and "complex" are no accident.

Fundamentals are deceivingly simple. To the untrained eye, they look easy and boring. You can feel tempted to rush past them to spend more time on flashier skills. You can feel a need to get out of the "beginner phase" that most people associate them to. I urge you **not** to do either of these.

## Play the long game (reasonably)
You want to think of your skills as a **long-term game for your own sake**. What usually happens if you don't is the following:
1. You rush past fundamentals after you think you did them right once or twice
2. You feel more accomplished and good about yourself from "levelling up"
3. You try to add more on top of that, trying to maintain momentum
4. Eventually you fail, and realize that you have to go "back down" to clean up bad habits
5. You are demoralized and discouraged, feeling like you lost time and are worse than you thought, and give up

Overcompensating in the other direction isn't helpful either:
1. You drill through fundamentals endlessly, not moving past them until you can't get them wrong
2. Because you don't challenge yourself past them, they become isolated and passive knowledge
3. You feel stuck, and never "good enough" to have fun
4. You get bored and discouraged, and give up

The key, once again, is balance!

This ~30s clip introduces the concept that allows us to multitask effectively, applied to StarCraft II.

**TLDW:** the mental checklist is a to-do list you loop over as you multitask

For every game, the checklist is:
1. What information is available to me now?
2. What is my best possible action now?
3. Execute my best possible action now
4. Repeat

## Game-specific adaptations
Each game may answer these differently, but there's common patterns.

### What information is available to me now?
Your game may send you the following types of sensory input: visual, auditory, tactile.

#### Visual
Information may be shared across:
1. **UI:** information about the game
2. **Viewport:** the game itself

Below, UI is marked blue; everything else is the viewport.
![UI vs Viewport]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/ui-panel-vs-viewport.png)

#### Auditory
Information may be shared across:
1. **SFX/music:** context-specific music or effects
2. **Narrator:** context-specific instructions
3. **Screen Readers:** accessibility

#### Tactile
Information may be shared across:
1. **Haptics:** context-specific vibrations

### What is my best possible action now?
Depends heavily on game knowledge and experience. Although first-hand experience tends to stick better, you can expand these from any match. Learn from watching others, browse existing tutorials, etc; just be mindful of not "theory crafting" too much. Remember to play the game too!

You should prioritize individual game skills too. The [StarCraft II Pyramid](https://tl.net/forum/sc2-strategy/253300-the-starcraft-pyramid) is a great example.

![SC2 Pyramid](http://i.imgur.com/dEd66.png)

### Execute my best possible action now
Mechanical execution of a decision after it has been chosen. The "how" depends on the game, but generally you'll have some combination of:
1. Mouse action(s)
2. Keyboard action(s)
3. Eyes action(s)

## Effective Practice
### Set some time aside to just play
Helps to avoid burnout. Play freely, whether it leverages what you practiced or not. Have fun and enjoy the game!

### Structure your time accordingly
Let's say you can only practice 20m per day. You can divide this time into however many blocks you want, and target specific drills during each one. Short, consistent, deliberate practice sessions beat the opposite every time.

### Use focused drills where appropriate
Generally, it's better to focus on one thing well than focus on many things poorly. Although not everything can be practiced in isolation, it is good to identify what can and prepare for it.

### Make it real
Drills and practice games should try to reinforce some skill that was or will be useful in a real game.

### Set up immediate corrective feedback
You want to avoid "winging it" as much as possible. For whatever can be constrained in your game through a practice match or similar mechanism, use it. It's most effective to stop a bad habit before it forms.

### Leverage your game's practice tools
Single player/AI matches, pausing and saving games, and other tools may be available to help you practice. You may also be able to create some of your own!

### Stop when you lose focus
Practice doesn't make perfect, it makes permanent. Sloppy practice in, sloppy games out, and viceversa.

## Common Gotchas
### Focusing on APM over understanding/reading the game
Execution, when practiced well, helps you do more/faster. It doesn't help you know what (not) to do.
[Slow is smooth, and smooth is fast](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/slow_is_smooth,_smooth_is_fast). The seemingly faster task from your perspective may not be the most efficient one.

### Learning a hotkey before doing its action manually
Hotkeys are a **mechanical optimization** of something. You need to understand the context of what that is to determine how important it is to your game, and whether a hotkey's benefits outweigh its drawbacks. You have limited physical real estate in your keyboard, mouse, and hands. [YAGNI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_aren%27t_gonna_need_it) most hotkeys most of the time, so spend time on those that matter most for what you want.

### Not jumping "in and out" of tasks
You do not want to get mentally stuck on any individual task. Doing so breaks your mental checklist and multitasking, and is guranteed to lead to worse decisions that will snowball during the match. There's a difference between choosing to do a similar action repeatedly and tunneling on something blindly.

### Not configuring your game properly
This can look different based on context. Impractical/uncomfortable hotkeys, scattered UI layouts, etc. Not all games will let you tune everything, but you should try to set yourself up for success where you can.
