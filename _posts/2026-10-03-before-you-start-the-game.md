## Why do we play games?
Here's a short clip from an excellent talk MTG Head Designer [Mark Rosewater](https://markrosewater.tumblr.com/) gave at GDC 10 years ago. For now, just these ~2 min are enough, but I'd urge you to watch the whole thing when you get a chance. It is a masterclass in storytelling and being passionate about what you do.

**TLDW:** broadly, we play games to
1. **experience** something
2. **express** something
3. **prove** something

These are different definitions of "fun" for different players (or even the same player at different times). No answer is universally better than the others. The point is to be **aware** of what it is **you** are looking for in a game!

## Why does that matter?
Knowing what you want out of a game helps you:
1. Choose what game you play
2. Choose how you play it

Listen to your gut, and do not fall into an [analysis paralysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_paralysis) trap. You can adjust along the way.

## Be curious
We are neither as different nor as similar to one another as we think. Chances are, there is someone out there that wants to play the same game as you, in a similar way, and for a similar reason. Before the internet, you needed to be very lucky with your "environment RNG"'s alignment to your interests. Although it is now easier to find your tribe online, face-to-face time is still just as valuable as ever.

You may find even more value if you explore a bit beyond your currently known interests as well!

## Adjust your expectations

In this ~1m clip, [Guthrie Govan](https://www.instagram.com/guthrie.govan) shares a common expectation trap that hobby newcomers face in the form of unrealistic expectations.

**TLDW:** you want to find a compromise between the shiny cool new thing you want and *think* you can do vs what you actually *can* do.

You must learn to walk before you can run. This doesn't mean that you won't be able to run, or that the learning processes will be identical. Patience, curiosity, and consistency are your best bet to grow.

## Practice vs Play
Practice is how you move from what you can do towards what you want to do. You want to find a sweet spot between attentiveness and flexibility. Don't just mindlessly go through the motions, but don't be so rigid as to let perfectionism limit your progress and enjoyment.

Playing is what you ultimately want to do, and what practice should aim towards. Their connection won't always be obvious, but both are necessary. Too little practice and your playing becomes bland and limited. Too little playing and you're just taking self-imposed standardized tests. Balance is key.

## The Bigger Picture
Being balanced with your time doesn't only apply in-game. Although games may offer a tempting escape from difficult emotions or circumstances in your real life, neither is separate from the other. If you keep an eye out on your mental/emotional state, you will:
* Have a better time inside and outside the game
* Be a better person to yourself and to others around you

It's not always easy and it will always demand effort from you. As with any skill, more (and better) practice will help!
